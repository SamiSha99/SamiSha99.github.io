This board game was made as part of my Game Design class in Tiltan, in this class we had to develop 3 game prototypes, then take one of them and fully complete it into a fully fledged iteration, Game of Castles is the one we went with.

Made together with Mohammad Alem, Ghanem Bhouti and Simon Shakkour.

Our main inspiration was [A Game of Thrones: The Board Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Game_of_Thrones:_The_Board_Game), it's a great game but it has a lot of rules to learn and a single game can easily take hours. We wanted something more fast paced and simpler, while still keeping what makes it fun: secret orders, fighting over land and outsmarting your friends.

## How it Plays

Each of the 4 players leads one of the four houses: Ashford, Nightbloom, Drakemoor and Ironhelm. Every house starts in its own corner of the board with a Castle and 4 Troopers, and over 8 rounds they expand, fight each other and try to control as much of the board as they can.

The board has 25 tiles: normal tiles, a Castle Tile in each corner, Bonus Tiles that give more resources and Water Tiles that nobody can move onto. Each tile can only hold up to 3 units at a time.

Every round your Castle recruits a free Trooper, but if someone takes your Castle Tile, you lose the castle and that free Trooper for the rest of the game.

Every round, players also place Action Tokens face down on the tiles they control, March to move and attack, Defense for an extra +1 when someone attacks that tile, and Mustering to recruit or upgrade units. Then everyone flips them one at a time, and battles are decided by rolling a die for each unit and adding their power on top.

## Initial Idea

We had to work in teams of up to 4 and make prototypes that are actually playable, using paper, pens, dice and so on. We came up with 3 different board games:

A party game where you and 3 other players go through a trap filled dungeon to get an artifact, you move using numbered cards and can split up through different paths. Once someone gets the artifact they have to escape, the other players can go after them and steal it, but the dungeon starts collapsing and forces everyone to leave.

A strategy game like Catan but with fog of war, you explore the board to find resources, make more troopers and fight. You win by destroying another player's main base, which takes them out of the game, or by beating a very strong kingdom in the middle that needs a lot of resources to take down.

And finally, Game of Castles, we picked this game because we found its concept the most interesting and our teacher Dor Ben Dor adviced us to expand this one in particular for having the most potential.

## My Work on the Project

I worked a lot on designing and balancing the game flow, playing it again and again and changing things after every playtest. I also worked heavily on the rules and wrote the first draft of the rulebook.

We added and cut a lot of mechanics along the way, the biggest one being the Support Token.

### The Support Token

The game used to have 4 Action Tokens instead of 3, the fourth one was the Support Token, which let your units on nearby tiles add their power to a battle. It allowed for some interesting plays, but in practice it caused "Turtling", where players stay passive, play very defensively and win by wearing out their opponents. Since any tile that gets attacked could be supported by the tiles around it, attacking was almost impossible, and some games ended with everyone tied.

We worked on this token for a long time, first we limited it to once per turn, then to twice per game, then to only once per game. But it went from useful to useless really quickly, and we couldn't find a middle ground to keep it.

So we went back to the drawing board, and the idea that stuck with us was a shop. That's where Research Points and Cards came from, you earn points from the tiles you hold and spend them on cards, and they replaced the Support Token completely.

From there it was mostly balancing numbers and adding limits to stop players from hoarding points or snowballing, like losing half your points if you start a round with 15 or more, and only being able to play 3 cards per round.

### Losing Your Castle

Losing your castle was a mechanic we kept until the last 2 weeks before the project's finals. We wanted it to give players a sense of weakness, you can conquer and expand all you want, but you have to keep your castle safe from every direction or else you lose everything.

And when I say everything, I mean ++literally everything++, you were out of the game and your whole region was open for anyone to invade.

Kicking a whole player out sounds awesome, but sadly it caused a snowball for the 2 players next to them, since both could expand into the empty region. Meanwhile, the player in the opposite corner has to watch with sad dry eyes, knowing they now have to go through two players that keep getting bigger. In other words, kicking a player out was killing two birds with one stone, the player across the board from them falls behind too, leaving the player across from you as your only real rival, since they can expand into the new region as well.

Since expanding is so rewarding, and tiles = points and points = winning, we found this to be a big flaw. So we changed the rule, instead of losing the game, you only lose the free Trooper your castle gives you every round. This puts the player on limited resources, but it's still hard for others to just take their tiles since their Troopers are still there defending them. And on the bright side, the player can keep playing for the rest of the game and still has a chance to win, even after losing such a strong mechanic.

## Final Thoughts

Making a board game was a cool introduction to game design, I had to think a lot about how rules are written and how to make them clear for players, which is why the rulebook ended up with its own Almanac for every term and card.

The game is fully playable, get yourself 3 friends and some free time, give the rulebook a read and try it out!
